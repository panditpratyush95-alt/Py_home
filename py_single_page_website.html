Frequently asked questions.

Quick answers to common questions about downloading, setting up and using py.

What is py?

py is a personal AI workspace designed to help with coding, creating, automation and exploring ideas. Depending on your setup, it can use supported cloud AI providers or a supported local model.

How do I download py?

Use any “Download py ZIP” button on this page. The file is hosted on Google Drive, so your browser may open a Drive page before the download starts.

What should I do after downloading?

Find py.zip in your browser’s Downloads folder, extract it, then open the included SETUP.txt and follow its instructions.

Do I need my own API key?

If you use a cloud AI provider, you may need to configure your own provider API key. Follow the setup instructions for the provider you choose, and never share your key publicly or commit it to a public repository.

Can I use py with a local AI model?

py’s project documentation describes support for local AI workflows. Availability depends on the model and dependencies you install; consult SETUP.txt before configuring a local model.

Does local AI mean every feature works offline?

Not necessarily. A local model can handle supported model tasks on your machine, but cloud providers, downloads, or other integrations may still require an internet connection.

Are my API keys and account data included in the ZIP?

The setup page says not to expect personal API keys, old account databases or private chat history to be included. Configure credentials and initialize accounts safely on your own machine.

Why can’t I see the ZIP in Downloads?

Wait a moment and check your browser’s download list. If it has not appeared, click a download button again. This website cannot verify whether your browser completed the download.

Where do I find installation instructions?

Extract the ZIP and open SETUP.txt in the top-level project folder. It contains the project’s setup guidance, prerequisites and first-run notes.

Can I keep the desktop and web projects separate?

Yes. The included setup guidance specifically says to keep py oraganised and Py organised Web as separate projects.

Still stuck? Recheck SETUP.txt first; it is the source of truth for your downloaded project version.
